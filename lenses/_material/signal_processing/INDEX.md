# Signal-Processing lens — corpus index

**Entry point.** Grep this file, then open ONLY the paper you need. Every PDF has a `.txt` twin.

This lens has **no book** — its two intended texts (Jelinek, *Statistical Methods for Speech
Recognition*; Kay, *Fundamentals of Statistical Signal Processing*) are commercial and were not
obtained. What is held is the **backbone**: the primary papers the field's instruments come from.

| source | who | what it backs |
|---|---|---|
| **Shannon (1948), *A Mathematical Theory of Communication*** (Bell System Technical Journal, 55pp) | Bell Labs | The information-theoretic floor, sampling, and why a channel has a capacity — F1 (characterise the noise before claiming a signal) and F7 (aliasing) |
| **Rabiner (1989), *A Tutorial on Hidden Markov Models*** (Proc. IEEE, 30pp) | Bell Labs; the canonical HMM reference | F5 — the observable is generated by a hidden state you never see. ⚠️ **OCR'd** (see note) |
| **Welch & Bishop, *An Introduction to the Kalman Filter*** (UNC TR 95-041, 16pp) | UNC Chapel Hill | F4 — carry a running state estimate with explicit uncertainty, updated by evidence |
| **Bishop & Welch, SIGGRAPH 2001 Course 8** (80pp) | as above | The long-form version, with the extended Kalman filter and worked numbers |

## Gaps

- **Kay, *Fundamentals of Statistical Signal Processing*, Vols I–II** — the detection/estimation
  standard. F2 (detection and estimation are different problems) and F3 (the matched filter) have no
  text behind them. **This is the one to buy.**
- **Jelinek, *Statistical Methods for Speech Recognition*** (MIT Press).

## OCR note — Rabiner

The available copy is a **pure scan**: 29 characters of text layer across 30 pages. It was OCR'd with
Tesseract 5.5 at 200dpi (45s), giving 147k characters. It is a **two-column paper and the OCR
interleaves the columns**, so sentences can run into each other across the gutter. Fine for grep and
for locating a passage; read the PDF page itself before quoting.
