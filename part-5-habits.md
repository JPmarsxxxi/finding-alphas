# Part V — Seven Habits of Highly Successful Quants (Ch. 32)

*A short, non-technical closing essay. No formulas, no signals — included for completeness. The one genuinely reusable tool is the risk–reward matrix.*

---

## The seven habits

1. **Work hard without realizing it** — steady daily effort beats raw talent; top performers find it fun ("can't believe I'm paid to do this"), not a grind.
2. **Ambitious long-term targets, attainable weekly goals** — pair a lofty target (PnL level, asset-class expertise) with day-sized tasks. (One researcher writes a Monday plan "as if sending it to the CEO," reviews it Friday.)
3. **Prioritize by risk & reward** — the matrix below.
4. **Stay curious** — every alpha has a finite, unpredictable shelf life; keep diversifying into new data/methods/asset classes. "Can that news article be turned into an alpha?"
5. **Value-added work — automate, automate, automate** — automate routine steps (data cleaning, viz, simulation, parameter tuning); reserve human time for idea formulation. (WorldQuant: 3 months/alpha → ~1/day after automation.)
6. **Make sensible changes, beware overfitting** — every change needs an economic rationale, not knob-twisting; hold out OOS data; run robustness tests. Overfitting risk ↑ with fewer data points (low-frequency data, small universes like currencies). See [[part-2c-overfitting-defense]].
7. **Form synergistic teams** — for idea incubation, workload sharing, research diversification, and the confidence to attempt big projects.

---

## The one reusable tool — risk–reward matrix (Habit 3)

Quants invest *time* rather than money. Score each task by risk and reward, then:

| | **Low risk** | **High risk** |
|---|---|---|
| **High reward** | **First priority** | pair with low-risk/low-reward (diversify) |
| **Low reward** | use to diversify high-risk bets | **avoid** |

Re-score periodically and keep adding new opportunities to the matrix.

> "Shoot for the moon. If you miss, you'll land among the stars."
